🧭 Planning Survival Loadouts

A loadout is simply the set of items you take with you for a particular job. The loadout screen lets you create as many as you like and give each one a name you will recognise, such as "Cave Run", "Long Hunt" or "Base Restock".

For each loadout you list the tools, weapons, food, medicine and materials you want to carry. When you head out, open the loadout and tick items off as you pack them. If you return with something missing, the list tells you exactly what to replace before the next trip.

Over time you build a library of proven kits. New players often forget rope, a torch or spare bandages — a saved loadout removes that problem entirely.

🏗️ Managing Base Projects

Large builds can feel overwhelming. The base project planner turns one enormous job into a checklist of small, finishable tasks: gather logs, lay the foundation, raise the walls, add the roof, place storage, secure the perimeter.

Each project can hold its own list, notes and material estimates. Mark tasks complete as you finish them and the progress bar fills in automatically, which makes it easy to see how close a build really is.

You can keep several projects open at once — a main base, a coastal outpost and a mountain shelter, for example — and switch between them without losing your place.

⚒️ Tracking Crafting Goals

The crafting section is a simple wish list with a materials counter. Add the item you want to make, list the parts it needs, and note how many of each part you already have.

The toolkit shows what is still missing, so a shopping trip into the forest becomes a focused errand instead of a wandering search. When every part is collected, mark the goal as complete and it moves to your finished list.

🕳️ Bunker Expedition Logs

Bunkers and caves are the heart of any island expedition, and it is easy to lose track of which ones you have cleared. The expedition log gives every location a status: not found, discovered, explored, looted.

Add a short note for each entry — the entrance you used, the items you found, the route you took and whether it is worth returning to. Months later, that note saves you an hour of walking in circles.

🗺️ Exploration Notes

This is your personal map in words. Save landmarks, resource-rich areas, safe camp spots, dangerous zones and the routes that connect them. You can group notes by region and search them instantly when you need to remember where that cave entrance was.

Exploration notes are also useful for planning: mark the places you have not visited yet and work through them one at a time.

📊 Progression Analytics

The analytics screen turns your notes into a simple picture of progress: how many projects you have completed, how many bunkers you have explored, how many crafting goals are finished and how your activity changes from week to week.

Nothing complicated is required from you. The numbers come from the lists you already keep, so the charts fill in as you go. It is a motivating way to see how far your character has come since the first day on the beach.
